Frequently asked questions

Short answers with links to the full detail. If your question is not here, email tenmokusan@gmail.com.

Do I need to book to visit?

No. The Zen garden is open daily, 8:00–17:00, and admission is ¥300 paid into an honour box. You can simply arrive.

Booking is required for Genjū retreats, Zen experiences, temple camping and formal prayer services. See Booking & fees.

Will anyone be there to receive me?

Usually not. On ordinary days the garden is unattended. There is no ticket desk, no guide and no shop. You pay into the box and walk in.

How long does it take from Tokyo? Can I do it as a day trip?

The route is: JR Chūō Main Line to Kai-Yamato Station 甲斐大和, then about 15 minutes by Kōshū City bus to the Tenmoku 天目 terminus, where the temple is.

The buses are infrequent and the last bus back is early. Check the timetable and note your return time before you settle in. See Getting here.

Can I sit zazen without booking?

Yes. The monthly zazen on the last Sunday of every month needs no reservation: morning service 6:50, zazen 7:00–8:00, tea 8:00–8:30, offering ¥300.

The difficulty is arriving by 6:50 on the first bus —

Do I need experience? Do I need to be a Buddhist?

No to both. Beginners are explicitly welcome at the monthly sitting. Nothing is asked of your religious beliefs, and you will not be asked to convert.

I cannot sit cross-legged on the floor.

Tell us when you book, or before the sitting starts. A chair or a kneeling posture can be arranged. Sitting in pain is not the point.

Is English spoken?

The temple operates in Japanese. Written English by email is the most reliable channel: tenmokusan@gmail.com

Can I pay by credit card?

Not at the temple. Admission, goshuin and temple items are cash only, and there is no ATM nearby. Bring coins and small notes.

What are the sleeping arrangements on a two-day retreat?

A shared room, sleeping on the floor, separated by gender — and you bring your own sleeping bag. Capacity is 11.

  • Futon rental: ¥2,000
  • Private room at the guesthouse by the gate: ¥4,500 per person, room only

We say this clearly because it is the single most common surprise for overseas guests. Full detail: Staying overnight.

Is there a private room inside the temple?

No. The only private option is the guesthouse at the temple gate (¥4,500, no meals). Request it when you apply.

Can I camp at the temple?

Yes — one group per night, so you will have the grounds to yourselves. Offering ¥10,000 plus ¥1,000 per person. Check-in 13:00–16:00, check-out 11:00. Firewood free; water and toilets available 24 hours; free Wi-Fi.

Condition: staying overnight obliges you to join the next morning’s service and rock zazen. See Staying overnight.

Can I see the painting of the figure holding a cross?

Not on an ordinary day — the treasure hall is closed and the figure is not on general display.

Two points of access: a special goshuin issued on two days in November (¥800), and the soba festival on 7–8 November 2026, when treasures are aired and the head priest gives a talk daily 12:00–12:30.

Research enquiries are welcome: Treasures & history.

Can I take photographs?

The garden is generally photographed by visitors. For anything beyond that — halls, interiors, treasures — please ask the temple in advance. Do not photograph people sitting zazen, and do not photograph graves.

Is there food? Is there anywhere to eat?

There are no shops, restaurants or vending machines at the bus stop. Bring your own food and water.

Shōjin ryōri 精進料理 (temple vegetarian food) is available only as a booked experience (食作法, “meal forms”), not as a walk-in meal.

What happens if it rains?

Rock zazen moves indoors, into the hall or the Kaikan. The decision is made on the day. The garden itself remains open in any weather — but wet granite is slippery, so wear proper shoes.

Can children come?

The Genjū retreats are for junior-high-school age and above. The garden itself has no age restriction, but it is a rock slope with steps and drops — keep young children close.

Is there Wi-Fi and power?

Yes, for retreat participants and temple-camping guests: free Wi-Fi and power outlets, and an indoor space called the “Genjū Net Café” 幻住ネットカフェ that camping guests may use.

Is there a hot spring?

Tenmokuzan Onsen 天目山温泉 is nearby; retreat guests pay ¥600 separately.

Is the temple wheelchair accessible?

The Zen garden is a slope of granite boulders with steps and uneven ground, and is not suitable for wheelchairs or for visitors who cannot manage rough terrain.

Indoor sitting in the hall or Kaikan may be possible as an alternative — please contact the temple in advance so it can be arranged rather than improvised.

How do I get a goshuin 御朱印?

Six standard designs, ¥300 each. When the head priest is at the temple they are brushed by hand; when he is away, pre-written sheets are available from the seal box on a self-service basis. Special versions and postal orders are listed on Visiting.

Zazen & Zen practice

Rock zazen 岩坐禅 means what it says: you sit on a granite boulder, outdoors, with the mountain around you and nothing between you and the weather. Monks have sat on these rocks since 1348. Beginners sit on them too.

1. Monthly zazen 定例坐禅会

The last Sunday of every month. No reservation. Beginners welcome.

6:50Morning service 朝課 (chanting in the hall)
7:00 – 8:00Zazen 坐禅
8:00 – 8:30Tea 茶礼 sarei
  • Offering: ¥300. There is no fee as such; you leave an offering.
  • Sitting is normally rock zazen in the Zen garden. In poor weather it moves indoors.
  • No experience is assumed. If you have never sat before, come to this one.
  • Getting there for 6:50 is the hard part. Check the first bus from Kai-Yamato Station carefully —

Conditions

  • Age: junior-high-school age and above
  • Capacity: 15 people per retreat
  • Application deadline: midnight on the Sunday two weeks before the retreat
  • Minimum numbers: a retreat with four or fewer applicants is cancelled
  • Facilities: free Wi-Fi and power outlets are available

Accommodation for two-day retreats

Overnight participants sleep inside the temple, in a shared room separated by gender, on the floor — and you bring your own sleeping bag. Futon rental is ¥2,000. A private room at the guesthouse by the temple gate is available at a participant rate of ¥4,500.

This is the most common surprise for overseas guests, so please read Staying overnight in full before you apply.

What participants receive

  • A reiseki-bukuro 霊石袋 — a pouch containing mica-bearing stone gathered in the temple grounds
  • Returning participants (second visit onward) also receive a Tenmokusan towel 天目山タオル

3. Zen experiences (by reservation)

Bookable sessions for individuals and groups. Each runs from about 60 minutes. Maximum 20 people. Advance reservation required.

  • Rock zazen 岩坐禅 — sitting in the Zen garden
  • Zazen in the main hall or the Kaikan — the indoor alternative
  • Sutra copying / Buddha drawing 写経・写仏
  • Samu 作務 — work practice; in this temple, cleaning the wooden floors by hand
  • Soba making 蕎麦打ち — see the note on soba in Treasures & history
  • Meal forms 食作法 — eating shōjin ryōri 精進料理 (temple vegetarian food) in the manner used by the monks
  • Kenchin soup class 建長汁 — learning to make the soup named after Kenchōji
  • Uchiwa goshuin うちわ御朱印 — a temple seal brushed onto a round fan
  • Treasure talk and guided tour of the grounds

To arrange one, see Booking & fees.

What sitting here actually involves

  • You sit cross-legged on a cushion, or on a rock, and stop moving. That is the whole instruction.
  • Sitting outdoors at 1,050 m means wind, insects in summer, and real cold outside summer. Dress for it. See Etiquette & what to bring.
  • No physical flexibility requirement. If you cannot sit cross-legged, say so — a chair or a kneeling posture can be arranged.
  • Nothing is asked of your beliefs. You are not required to be a Buddhist, and you will not be asked to become one.

A note on “Sanctuary of Zazen”

The temple holds a Japanese registered trademark on the phrase 坐禅の聖地®, registered on 17 December 2021. An English rendering such as “Sanctuary of Zazen” is a translation for explanation only; the registration covers the Japanese-language mark in Japan.

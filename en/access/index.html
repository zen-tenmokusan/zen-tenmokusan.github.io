Getting here

One train line, one bus. The bus runs four times a day up to the temple and five times a day back down. Plan the return before you plan the arrival.

Warning for satellite navigation and map apps.
There are two temples called Seiunji in Kōshū City. The other — Tenryūzan Seiunji 天龍山栖雲寺, of the Myōshinji branch — is in Enzan Yuzuriki, a different valley.
Enter the postal code 409-1201 or the full address above, not the name alone.

The route in one line

JR Chūō Main Line → Kai-Yamato Station 甲斐大和 → bus, about 14 min → “Tenmoku (Seiunji)” 天目（栖雲寺）, the last stop → arrive

The temple is at the bus terminus. There is no walk from the stop.

Limited express trains do not stop at Kai-Yamato

Azusa あずさ and Kaiji かいじ pass straight through. Kai-Yamato is the only station between Ōtsuki and Enzan with a passing loop, and the limited expresses use it to overtake local trains — they do not stop.

If you have a rail pass or want the fastest train, you have two sensible options:

Ride the local train all the wayShinjuku → Takao → Kai-Yamato. ¥2,090, about 1 h 50 min to 2 h 15 min, one change at Takao.
Take the Kaiji to Enzan 塩山, and the bus from thereThe same Kōshū City bus runs all the way from Enzan Station South Exit to the temple — about 65 minutes. One change instead of two.

Kai-Yamato Station — what is and is not there

It is an unstaffed station. Please read this before you travel.

  • No staffed ticket office (midori-no-madoguchi), and no ticket machine — the machine was removed in December 2020. A boarding-certificate dispenser is provided instead.
  • A simple Suica reader is installed, so IC cards work for the train.
  • There is a waiting area. The platforms are reached by stairs.
Buy your return ticket before you leave Tokyo, or be ready to use an IC card. You cannot buy a ticket at Kai-Yamato.

Michi-no-Eki Kai-Yamato 道の駅甲斐大和, about 15 minutes’ walk from the station, has a shop and a restaurant serving hand-cut soba.
April–November 9:00–18:00, open daily; December–March 9:00–17:00, closed Wednesdays.

B｜Eiwa Kōtsū mountain bus — seasonal, weekends only

The Daibosatsu Kamihikawa-tōge line, run by Eiwa Kōtsū 栄和交通, climbs past the temple to the Mt. Daibosatsu trailhead. Get off at Tenmoku.

  • Weekends and public holidays only, from the third Saturday of April to the second Sunday of December.
  • It does not run from mid-December to mid-April. In winter, use service A.
  • On weekdays it runs only on specific dates listed in its own timetable.
  • Eiwa Kōtsū: 0553-26-2344. Groups of 15 or more must contact them in advance.

Services are cancelled in severe weather. On 21 September 2026 the Daibosatsu line was suspended for a typhoon forecast. If a typhoon is coming, check before you travel.

The fare, on the city bus

¥300 per ride, flat — the same whatever the distance. A one-day pass is ¥1,000. Children below school age travel free.

Carry cash. The city’s published information does not say whether IC cards are accepted on this route, and we have not been able to confirm it. Assume you will pay in coins.

Flag stops

Between Funagō-bashi 舟郷橋 and Tenmoku (Seiunji), the city bus runs as a free-boarding section (jiyū jōkō kukan 自由乗降区間): you may get on or off anywhere along the road, not only at marked stops. Tell the driver where you are going.

Enquiries about the city bus: Kōshū City, Urban Development Division, 0553-32-5072.

By taxi

Two companies serve Kai-Yamato Station:

Katsunuma Kankō Taxi 勝沼観光タクシー0553-44-1432
Eiwa Kōtsū Taxi 栄和交通タクシー0120-77-0413 (toll-free within Japan)

Assume you will need to telephone. The station is unstaffed and both companies are based elsewhere; no public source says a taxi waits at the station.

The temple gives the drive as about 15 minutes.

By car

  • Katsunuma IC 勝沼インター (Chūō Expressway) — about 20 minutes
  • Ōtsuki IC 大月インター (Chūō Expressway) — about 30 minutes

Coming from Tokyo, the temple recommends leaving at Ōtsuki and taking the ordinary road: about the same time, and cheaper in tolls. Going on to Katsunuma means doubling back.

On National Route 20, turn at the Keitokuin-iriguchi 景徳院入口 signal toward Tenmoku — left from Katsunuma, right from Ōtsuki.

Parking — free

Coaches up to 12 m can park and turn.

Two official sources differ on the number of car spaces: the temple says about 7, the Kōshū City tourism association says 10 (free), plus one coach space (free).

The turning area is also the city bus’s layover and turning point. If a bus arrives at the same time, please make room for each other.

On foot, from the station

The Ryūmonkyō Gorge trail 竜門峡ハイキングコース starts at Kai-Yamato Station and ends at Seiunji. Two to two and a half hours, following the Hikawa valley upstream.

The temple recommends this as the finest way to arrive. Walking back down takes about 70 minutes.

Yamato Tenmokuzan Onsen — the hot spring

The nearest bath, and the one retreat guests use. It is also the bus stop before the temple.

  • 10:00–19:00 (last entry 18:00)
  • Closed Wednesdays, and over the New Year
  • ¥600 for adults from outside Kōshū City (¥400 for residents); ¥400 for primary-school children; free below school age
  • Alkaline simple spring, pH 10.2 · Tel 0553-48-2000

Before you set out

  • The temple is high in the mountains and colder than Tokyo all year.
  • Bring cash. Admission, goshuin and temple items are cash only, and there is no change machine. You cannot buy a train ticket at Kai-Yamato either.
  • There are no shops at the Tenmoku bus stop. The nearest are at Michi-no-Eki Kai-Yamato, back down by the station.

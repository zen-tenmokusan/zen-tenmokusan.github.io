Visiting

There is no ticket window. You walk up, drop ¥300 into a wooden box, and go in. On most days there will be nobody else on the slope — only granite, moss, and whatever the weather is doing at 1,050 metres.

Hours and admission

OpenDaily, 8:00 – 17:00 (stone garden and cemetery)
Admission¥300 per person
How to paySelf-service. Put the fee in the honour box at the entrance. There is no attendant.
ReservationNot required for the garden
Time neededAbout 30 minutes to walk the garden at an unhurried pace

Please bring coins. There is no change machine, no card reader and no one to make change.

What you are walking through

The Zen garden (zentei 禅庭, also called the stone garden, sekitei 石庭) is a Place of Scenic Beauty designated by Yamanashi Prefecture (meishō 名勝, designated 31 March 1979 as Seiunji Teien 棲雲寺庭園). It is not a raked gravel garden of the kind found in Kyoto temple courtyards. It is the mountainside itself: an expanse of granite boulders, some the size of rooms, threaded by paths and steps.

This is the temple. When Gōkai Honjō 業海本浄 founded Seiunji in 1348, he took the rock field as the training ground rather than clearing it to build on. Seven hundred years later it is still used that way. Three forms of practice are named after its features:

  • Rock zazen 岩坐禅 — sitting on the boulders, in the open
  • Cave zazen 岩窟坐禅 — sitting inside the rock hollows
  • Zazen beneath the trees 樹下坐禅

If you would like to sit rather than only look, see Zazen & Zen practice.

Points to look for

The Jizō cliff carving 地蔵磨崖仏
A figure of Jizō carved directly into the rock face within the grounds.

The kuri 庫裏 (temple kitchen-quarters)
Rebuilt in 1592 on land granted by Tokugawa Ieyasu after the temple was burned in 1582, and still standing — over four centuries old. A Yamanashi Prefecture designated cultural property. It was used as a location in the feature film Ōi, Ōi 『おーい、応為』, released in Japan on 17 October 2025.

The Takeda cemetery
Seiunji is the memorial temple of the Takeda clan. See Treasures & history.

The view
At 1,050 m, in a valley that the founder chose because it reminded him of Mt. Tianmu near Hangzhou.

Rest house and toilets

Sekitei Kaikan 石庭会館, next to the garden, is a free rest house managed by Kōshū City. It has toilets.

  • Typical opening: approximately 9:00–16:00

There are no shops, no vending machines and no restaurants at the bus stop. Bring water and something to eat. See Etiquette & what to bring.

Currently closed / limited

The treasure hall is closed. The temple’s cultural properties are not on general display at present.

  • To ask about viewing a specific cultural property, contact the temple in advance: tenmokusan@gmail.com / 0553-48-2797
  • During 1 February – 31 May 2026, viewing of the temple’s cultural properties was suspended while objects were lent to the exhibition Zen Buddhist Culture of Yamanashi 「山梨の禅宗文化」 at the Yamanashi Prefectural Museum.

The Zen garden remains open every day, including during periods when the treasures are unavailable. If you are coming for the garden, nothing above affects you.

The hidden Buddha, Takeda Fudō 武田不動尊, is a hibutsu — an image not shown to the public except on specific occasions.

Goshuin 御朱印 (temple seals)

Six standard goshuin are available, ¥300 each:

  • Hōkan Shaka Nyorai 宝冠釈迦如来
  • Jizō cliff carving 地蔵磨崖佛
  • Sobatō Kannon 蕎麦祷観音
  • Takeda Fudōson 武田不動尊
  • Marishiten 摩利支尊天
  • Myōken Bosatsu 妙見大菩薩

When the head priest is at the temple, these are brushed by hand. When he is away, pre-written sheets are available from the seal box on a self-service basis.

Special versions:

  • Sobatō Kannon, coloured, double-page spread — ¥800 (postal order accepted)
  • Marishiten, watermarked, on its own mount only — ¥500
  • Manichaean figure special goshuin, double-page spread — offering ¥800, issued on two days only in November
